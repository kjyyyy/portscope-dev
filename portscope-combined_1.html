01 The Sequoia Thesis Applied
Sequoia Capital “Services: The New Software” · March 2026

Don’t sell the tool. Sell closed books.

Julien Bek’s thesis: the next trillion-dollar company won’t sell software tools — it will sell the work itself. Every model improvement makes a tool-seller vulnerable and a work-seller more powerful. Here’s how that maps to family offices.

“A company might spend $10K a year for QuickBooks and $120K on an accountant to close the books. The next legendary company will just close the books.”

— Julien Bek, Sequoia Capital · March 2026
❌ Copilot (the trap)

Sell access to the tool

Users log in, see a dashboard, and use AI to help them do the work themselves. You charge per seat. Every 6 months a better Claude update makes your interface a feature. You race model providers forever.

✓ Autopilot (the thesis)

Deliver the outcome directly

Capital calls processed. Books reconciled. Reports delivered. Clients barely touch any interface. You charge for the output — and every model improvement makes you faster, cheaper, and harder to displace.

Upcoming wire deadlines extracted automatically. One missed wire = LP default. This prevents it.

The value

When Portscope processes a capital call, it already extracts the due date and wire instructions. The Wire Calendar surfaces these automatically — no re-entry, no spreadsheet. A missed capital call wire can trigger LP default clauses. This feature eliminates that risk entirely.

⚠ Risk this prevents
Missing a capital call wire typically triggers a 2% penalty on the call amount and can result in loss of unfunded commitment rights — $17K penalty on an $847K call.

💸 Commitment Tracker & 12-Month Cash Flow Forecast

Unfunded commitments plus AI-estimated call pace = a 12-month liquidity picture. “We need $2.1M in liquid reserves for the next 12 months.”

The value

Most family offices don’t know their total unfunded commitment across all funds, let alone when it will be called. Portscope knows commitment size from NAV statements, knows what has been called from capital call documents, and can estimate the remaining draw schedule based on typical PE fund pacing. The result: a liquidity forecast that updates automatically every time a document is approved.

How the forecast is built
Unfunded commitment per fund (from NAV statements) × historical call pace for that fund vintage and strategy = projected quarterly calls. Sums to a 12-month bar chart of expected outflows plus any confirmed upcoming calls from processed capital call notices.

06 Pilot Onboarding
How the first 3 clients get started

White-glove, manual, by design

Don’t build self-serve onboarding for the MVP. Pilot clients are onboarded by you, manually, over 2–3 sessions. This is intentional — it earns trust before the product earns it on its own.

1

Discovery call (60 min) — before any code

Interview Sarah about the current workflow. Map every document type they receive, every fund admin, every entity. Ask to see 5–10 real capital call PDFs.

Leave with: Entity tree, fund list, email addresses fund admins send from, sample PDFs, and Sarah’s biggest pain points. This becomes your fine-tuning seed dataset and onboarding config.
Duration: 1 week including prep
2

Data setup — you do this for them

You build their entity tree. You create their fund records. You configure email forwarding: fund admin emails go to whitmore@docs.portscope.io.

Don’t make them do this. The moment onboarding becomes a form-filling exercise, you’ve lost the “we do the work” positioning. White-glove setup is part of the service.
Duration: 3–5 days, async
3

Live demo: extract a real document in the room

Book a 30-min call with Sarah. Ask her to forward a real capital call email live during the call. Watch it appear in the review queue together. Let her click Approve herself.

This is magic moment 1. Don’t show a recorded demo. Don’t use sample data. Her document. Her fund. Her approval. Her queue going to zero. Do this live.
Duration: 30 min · highest-impact session
4

Two-week shadow period — you watch, they operate

For two weeks, every auto-approved document is also reviewed by you internally. You’re a silent second pair of eyes. Catch errors before Sarah sees them.

Trust before automation. Two weeks of you quietly double-checking — then showing Sarah the accuracy metrics — builds the foundation for increasing auto-approval thresholds.
Duration: 2 weeks
5

First quarterly report delivery — the relationship moment

At the end of the first quarter, you generate the quarterly report. Sarah approves and clicks Send. James gets the email. He clicks the magic link. You follow up: “What did James say?”

This is when they stop thinking of you as a vendor. You delivered a quarterly close — the thing they used to dread — without them asking. Now ask for the referral.
Duration: End of quarter 1

LLM strategy — swap via .env

Provider-agnostic abstraction

A single LLM_PROVIDER env var routes to Ollama (local, free), OpenRouter (100+ models, pay-per-use), Anthropic, OpenAI, HuggingFace, vLLM, or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint. No DGX Spark required — start free with Ollama on any machine.

Object storage — local then prod

MinIO (dev) → S3 (prod)

MinIO runs in Docker Compose and exposes a fully S3-compatible API. No AWS account needed for development. In production, swap four env vars to point at real S3, Cloudflare R2, or DigitalOcean Spaces. Same code, zero changes.

Key infrastructure decisions No DGX Spark: LLM inference is provider-agnostic. Run Ollama free locally, upgrade to Claude/GPT-4o when needed.   Temporal: Durable workflows survive crashes; human-in-the-loop approval via signals (7-day wait, no polling).   pdfplumber: Primary PDF parser, clean text extraction for LLM prompts, with boto3/S3 download and local file fallbacks.
